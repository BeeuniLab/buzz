# Choice Quiz Story

Choice, quiz, and story playables should not become static cards. They need a simple interactive loop, clear progress, satisfying selection feedback, and a result or reveal that feels earned.

## Core Loop

- Present one clear prompt or situation at a time.
- Use two to four large touch choices, not many tiny options.
- Show progress: step dots, `2/5`, score, streak, reveal meter, mood, or branching state.
- End with a result, rating, reveal, mini scene, share-worthy punchline, or replay.
- Make each choice cause a visible reaction before advancing; otherwise the experience feels like a form.

## Presentation

- Make the subject visible with a central image, character, prop, scene, or animated symbol.
- Use short prompt copy. Put flavor in the scene and result, not in long instructions.
- Keep choices visually distinct and reachable. Use icons, colors, symbols, or small props when useful.
- For story/branching, show the consequence of the choice before moving on.
- Stage the result visually: spotlight, flip, curtain, meter burst, character reaction, or prop transformation.

## Feedback

- On choice: selected option lifts/scales, unselected choices dim, subject reacts, progress advances.
- Correct/incorrect quiz: use clear success/error states, but keep the flow forgiving.
- Reveal/meme: stage the reveal with a flip, wipe, spotlight, countdown, or bounce.
- Result: show a strong title, score/category, one visual, and a replay button.

## Variants

- **Quiz:** clear prompt, answer cards, right/wrong animation, streak or score, final rank.
- **Personality/result:** mood meter or trait tokens, playful subject reactions, strong result card.
- **Interactive story:** scene prop changes after each choice, short consequence line, final branch reveal.
- **Voting/social challenge:** two large sides, live-feeling meter, dramatic winner reveal.

## Avoid

- Do not create a static questionnaire page with no motion.
- Do not make the user read long paragraphs before the first tap.
- Do not use tiny radio buttons or desktop form controls.
- Do not overcomplicate branching; a short polished result beats a large unfinished tree.
- Do not use only text as payoff; every result needs a visual change or reveal.
